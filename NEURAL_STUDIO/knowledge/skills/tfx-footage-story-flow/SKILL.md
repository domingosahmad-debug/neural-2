# Footage selection and flow

Build an edit around character intention and action, not a bag of visually similar clips.

1. Establish main character, conflict, emotional arc and music phases. Write one sentence per phase explaining what changes for the viewer.
2. Split source into continuous camera angles. Make labeled contact sheets with source filename, exact timestamp, scene boundary and available handles. Review motion in shortlisted windows, not just the prettiest still.
3. Maintain a shot ledger: file/hash, source range, block offset, character(s), action, emotion, shot scale, motion vector, focal point, timeline range, narrative role, visible speech, source-audio policy, use count and confidence.
4. Choose a sequence with anticipation → action → contact → reaction. Put the strongest action, with enough setup to understand it, at the first highlight and climax. A final still of a finisher is not the whole payoff.
5. Link neighboring shots by pose, sword/hand geometry, screen direction, subject position, action causality or emotional reaction. A wide sword loading into its close-up works; a disconnected foot insert often does not. Design the transition only after the pair makes sense.
6. Avoid accidental repetition and long old-memory scenes. Brief flashbacks need an explicit narrative function. Do not show a character dead and then resume the same fight unless the temporal shift is deliberate and legible.
7. Reject visibly moving speech mouths without their matching synchronized dialogue in ALL music-only body shots, including sad/emotional edits, not just action sections. Check the entire selected range, not one thumbnail; captions, background music or unrelated voice-over do not fix silent lip movement. Keep complete synchronized intro/dialogue shots when authorized. Offscreen speech may accompany a listening/reaction image, but do not show another character visibly delivering the line.
8. Confirm safe subject crops and actual handle length at the intended speed. A continuous-shot layer must not conceal another source cut. Strong native camera movement may need little or no added motion.

For a creative replica, transfer pacing and visual relationships rather than duplicate every shot. User-authorized reuse of some shots is allowed; never use the finished reference as replacement source footage. When the brief has no reference, construct and prove the music/story map first.

Gate: explain each shot's role and why it follows the previous shot; show that all strong music peaks receive earned visual peaks. Repair selection before adding effects. Alternate versions must differ meaningfully in structure, not just shuffle equivalent clips.
